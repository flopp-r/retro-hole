# Retro Hole: handover notes

Why things are the way they are, the measured numbers, what is still to decide, and what has shipped. `AGENTS.md` says how it works; this says why.

## 1. What the owner asked for (24 November 2026)

A game like hole.io: a hole you steer; everything falls in if the hole is big enough; the hole levels up and gets bigger. An endless generated map with different areas (city, forest, "Jurassic Park" and so on); the further out, the harder (bigger) the objects. A very high level cap so a game can go on for a long time. The Retro Set look: pixelated voxels. An isometric view from above. On a GitHub page, with online play perhaps later.

## 2. What was agreed before building

Four choices, each with the trade-offs explained and a recommendation (24 November 2026):

| Question | Chosen | The other option |
|---|---|---|
| Drawing | 3D with an isometric camera and Retro Set's pixel filter (the recommendation) | 2D sprites: lighter, but things could not really tip into the hole, no real lighting, and a different look |
| Opponents | **Solo first, bots later** (not the recommendation, which was bots from the start) | bot holes that grow and can eat each other |
| Level cap | 1,000 levels (the recommendation) | 500, or no cap (rejected: the numbers eventually break down) |
| Areas in the first build | Village, forest and city | Jurassic park was offered and not chosen for the first build |

The third round (25 November 2026), also after explaining the options and a recommendation each:

| Question | Chosen | The recommendation, where it differed |
|---|---|---|
| How bots exist on an endless map | **Fixed bots across the map**, decided by the seed, simulated while their cell is loaded | Bots near you, made as needed (cheaper, and always someone your size). Fixed was chosen; see section 3 |
| How bots treat you | Hunt when clearly bigger, flee when smaller (the recommendation) | Only compete for food; always hunt the nearest hole |
| Being swallowed | Lose half your xp and respawn a little way off, protected for a few seconds (the recommendation) | Lose nothing; back to level 1 |
| New biomes | **All four**: Jurassic Park, desert, arctic, volcano | Jurassic Park, desert and arctic (the volcano needs lava ground, so a little more work) |

## 3. Design decisions and why

- **Size grows with distance, in a straight line** (`scaleAt(d) = 1 + d / 120 m`). The first idea, sizes that double every ring, would have put the far map millions of metres out, where the graphics numbers lose precision (things jitter). Linear growth keeps coordinates sane: even the level cap needs only about 500 km. A hole that has grown to match its surroundings meets the same world wherever it is. This is why the map can be "infinite" and the cap high at once.
- **The hole grows fast at first, slower later** (`CURVE` 0.6 in `radiusAt`). The first build grew by the same share every level (0.86%), which looked far too slow: after 10 levels the hole was 8% wider. The owner asked for faster growth (24 November 2026, after trying it). Now the first levels are 7% each, so ten levels is 1.7 times wider, while the cap is still 5,000 times the start. Because the world's things are sized to distance, a bigger hole just has to walk further out to meet things that suit it.
- **Hole size is on screen and constant:** the camera sits so many hole radii from the middle to the top (7.6), so the world scales round the hole. A level 1 hole and a level 1,000 hole look the same size on the screen; what changes is what they can eat.
- **Things stay inside cells and cells are made from the seed alone.** Nothing is stored for the map. The same seed gives the same map, which is also what online play needs (two players only have to share a number).
- **Things you eat grow back** when their cell is dropped and made again. The alternative (remember every eaten thing) would grow without end. The cost: you can regrow an area by leaving and coming back, which makes farming a spot possible. Accepted, since the xp of a thing falls as you outgrow it.
- **The hole is a cut-out in the ground**, drawn by a shader (every pixel inside the circle is thrown away), with a dark pit underneath. Things that fall pass below the ground and are only seen through the gap. This gives a pixel-exact round hole with no extra geometry and nothing to clip.
- **The hole is never hidden, and nothing blocks the camera.** A tall building in front of or over the hole would hide it in an isometric view. The hole has a yellow ring drawn over everything, and any thing too big to eat that is over the hole, in front of it or just beside that line dissolves (to 80%, in the pixel pattern of the post filter) while it is in the way. The first version cut a patch out of tall things only; the owner wanted the whole thing to dissolve when the hole is under it. It is a dissolve and not smooth see-through, because blending would need sorting of thousands of instanced boxes, and the dissolve matches the dithered look.
- **Big things don't block.** The hole passes under anything too big for it (as in hole.io). The alternative, solid obstacles, was not chosen.
- **A framework of plots, so everything is believable** (second round, at the owner's request). The map's square cells are now plots, each of one area and one kind (a farm, a clearing, a city block ...), and every kind has a layout built from the same tools: things in rows facing a road, fences round a garden, animals inside a pen, plants at the foot of trees, kerb furniture along a pavement, traffic in the lanes. A thing is only ever placed where it makes sense (see `AGENTS.md`). The first build scattered every thing at random across its area with only a size rule, which put cars off the road and houses at any angle. The cost is that a plot is the unit of the map: areas change at cell edges (about 32 m wide at the start, twice as wide in each ring), and every plot of one kind has the same recipe, so variety comes from the random choices within it.
- **Cities are a square street grid**, not the first build's radial roads and ring roads. Rows of buildings facing a street need straight edges and right angles, which a spiderweb cannot give. Roads run along cell edges, so a block is a cell with a pavement all round. Ring by ring the cells (and so the blocks) double in size and the streets stay in proportion; every second street of one ring continues into the next and the others end at the ring's edge, as side streets do. A road is as wide as the ring's scale, so through-streets widen at a ring edge. Buildings are low near the start (shops, terraces) and taller further out (flats from 120 m, offices 220 m, towers 400 m, skyscrapers 840 m, where a block is big enough for one).
- **Bots are fixed in the map, not made as needed** (the owner's choice). Whether a cell has a bot, and where and how big it is, comes from the seed alone, so it is the same each time, and two players online will see the same bots. A bot's size is the local scale times a factor, so wherever a hole of about the right size goes it meets bots about its own size. The costs: a bot does not keep its progress while you are away (its cell is dropped and made again at its starting size, as eaten things regrow), and bots only exist where cells are loaded.
- **Bots are kept fair, because a bot that grows like a perfect player would swallow a new player at once.** A bot earns 0.2 of the xp a thing gives you, weighs distance heavily when choosing food, moves at 0.6 of your speed (so you can outrun a bot up to about 1.4 times your size), and **stops growing**: at 1.4 times the size its place suits within 150 m of the start (so no bot near the start is much bigger than a new hole), and 3 times that size further out. A new game starts with 8 seconds of protection and a respawn with 4. The first test showed a bot reaching level 33 in 30 seconds and swallowing a standing player; these limits came from measuring that.
- **Mouse steering can be turned off** (M, or Mouse Steering in Settings), for playing on the keyboard with the pointer free. Saved with the settings.
- **A small hole far out is not trapped.** Everything out there is huge, and the hole's speed is in proportion to its size, so the walk home could take hours. The speed never falls below that of a hole a quarter the size the place suits, a hint says what to do after 6 seconds with nothing to eat, and the menu has Return to Start.

- **Quick Game, a second mode** (25 November 2026, the owner's request: "biomes dont get bigger further out, and you grow much faster"). Questions asked: what stops growing with distance (answer: everything, one fixed scale) and how long it lasts (answer: no limit, extremely fast size increase). Built as a mode switch in `world.js` (`setMode`), not a second world file, so the layouts, models, plots and bots are shared and the normal game's 666 checks still pass unchanged. Decisions: one scale and one cell size (ring 0 everywhere); areas are a jittered lattice of 360 m regions (the village round the start is at least 250 m); levels have no cap and need very little xp (0.12 xp plus 0.002 a level); the radius is `0.5 * (1 + 0.8 * (level - 1)) ^ 0.6`, so the hole is as big as the biggest buildings by about level 100; bots and rare things still scale with distance (bots up to 14 times by 1.5 km, `botScale`) so the far map stays more dangerous; a separate save (`retroHole.saveQuick`) so the modes never overwrite each other. **Known consequence the owner was warned of:** with one scale, nothing is bigger than the biggest buildings, so past about level 100 the hole outgrows everything and further levels are worth little; a hole past level 500 sees mostly dust. If that is not wanted, a cap or a bigger "boss" size for far things is the next decision (section 4).

## 4. Decisions waiting for the owner

0. **Quick Game, past level 100.** With one scale there is nothing bigger than the biggest buildings, so a hole past about level 100 has little left to eat. Options: a level cap (one number), a few much bigger far-out things (giants, whole volcanoes) that only appear far from the start in quick mode, or leave it as it is. Not decided.
1. **Bots, further:** more kinds of rival (a few that hunt only other bots, named champions, a bot that chases whoever is leading), team play, a longer scoreboard, an offscreen arrow to the nearest threat, bots that keep their progress (needs memory kept across cell reloads, which online play also wants).
2. **Online play.** The world needs only the seed, so two players share a number and then send moves; eaten things would be sent as (cell, index) pairs. Retro Set's relay (a Cloudflare Worker with Durable Objects) could be reused, but the rooms would hold about 10 players, not hole.io's hundreds, and nothing stops a modified copy cheating (the same as Retro Set). Needs: the relay, who can eat whom, and keeping eaten things in step. Bots are already deterministic from the seed, so two players would see the same ones (but their movement would have to be agreed or simulated by one side). Ask before building (rule 7 in `AGENTS.md`).
3. **More areas.** Jurassic Park, desert, arctic and volcano are in. Ideas for more: seaside and harbour, ruins and castle, swamp, farmland with a river, space station, candy land. An area is a `BIOMES` entry, a chooser in `plotKind()`, layouts in `LAYOUTS`, colours in `groundColour()` and its things in `TYPES`; adding one changes every map for the same seed.
4. **Cosmetics, a score table or a locker** in the style of Retro Set. Not designed yet.
5. **The installable app** (manifest, icons, service worker for offline), as Retro Set has.
6. **Music.** Only short sound effects exist.

## 5. Measured numbers

**Tests (25 November 2026):** `node tests/world.test.js`: 666 passed. `node tests/browser/run-all.js`: both files passed, repeated runs stable. All run in headless Chromium with software drawing, so no frame rate on a real graphics card or a phone has been measured. The game was never run on a real device.

**The climb (`node tools/sim-pace.js`).** An estimate for a steady player who sweeps the ground at 60% efficiency, found by making real map cells at the right size for each level. A real player pauses, takes wrong turns and wanders, so real times are longer (allow about half as fast again). Early levels are meant to be quick. The tool counts: things a hole of that size can eat per second (density of food times the area the hole sweeps, which is its width times its speed), the xp each is worth, and the xp a level needs. Some rows are uneven because the sample landed near a ring edge, where a cell is only partly in its ring, or on a plot with little food in it (a pond, a car park); read the trend.

| 10 | 1.7 m | 0.74 | 0.13 | 0.07 | 0.01 |
| 25 | 2.5 m | 0.66 | 0.19 | 0.08 | 0.04 |
| 50 | 4.0 m | 0.41 | 0.25 | 0.15 | 0.09 |
| 100 | 8.4 m | 0.33 | 0.16 | 0.51 | 0.33 |
| 200 | 25 m | 0.60 | 0.19 | 0.45 | 1.00 |
| 300 | 62 m | 0.58 | 0.24 | 0.54 | 1.9 |

History of the same estimate: first build 42 minutes to level 100 and about 63 hours to the cap; after the first feedback (faster curve, more food) 19 minutes and 24 hours; after the plot framework 37 minutes and 52 hours, retuned (density 1.8, xp 0.14) to 20 minutes and 29 hours; after the owner asked for many more objects everywhere (density 3.2, more street furniture, parked cars, denser forests and fields) the hole met so much more food that it fell to 10 minutes and 16 hours, so the xp a level needs was raised (0.14 to 0.27) to keep the pace the owner had approved: then 19 minutes and 32 hours. After the four new areas were added (the tool now samples all seven; the new ones have plenty of food) the same settings give about 20 minutes to level 100 and about 20 hours to the cap, so the level-100 pace the owner approved is kept and the cap is a little nearer. Bots do not change the estimate (it is for a player left alone). The numbers that set it are `CURVE`, `need()` and the flat part of `xpFor()`, `DENSITY` and the per-100 counts in the layouts, and `SPEED` in `src/game.js` (also in `tools/sim-pace.js`).

**Cost:** a cell takes about 1 ms to make. Views of every area at levels 30 to 280 were 38 to 80 draw calls and 85,000 to 185,000 triangles (700 to 1,650 things) in software rendering, more than before because the world is denser and the new areas' models are bigger; there is no frame rate measured on a real graphics card or phone, and a slow device should lower the pixel size in Settings. Typical objects a cell: villages 130 to 260, new areas 110 to 240 (all 42 kinds average 88 or more), forests 115 to 160, city blocks about 90. 193 kinds of thing. The budget is 14,000 things made at once. Bots add up to 12 more holes in the ground shader and a few DOM labels.

**Quick Game's climb (`node tools/sim-pace.js 0.6 quick`, 25 November 2026, an estimate for a steady player at 60% efficiency):** level 10, 3.5 m wide, 0.1 minutes; level 25, 6.1 m, 0.2; level 50, 9.2 m, 0.3; level 100, 14 m, 0.5; level 200, 21 m, 1.1; level 300, 27 m, 2.1; level 500, 36 m, 4.3; level 1,000, 55 m, 16.5 minutes. Against about 20 minutes to level 100 in the normal game. Real play is slower; the bots and the first minute (finding food while small) are not in the estimate.

## 6. Ideas

Boost button (with a cost), power-ups, a minimap or a compass to the start, rare golden things, things that react as they fall (cars honk, cows moo), rivers and lakes, weather, day and night, daily challenges, a score for the furthest you have been.

## 7. Known limits

- **Dust.** A hole much bigger than the cells it sits in (for example a level 500 hole at the start) does not make things smaller than 5% of its radius, and makes at most the nearest 14,000 things, so the area near it can look bare. By design: it has outgrown it.
- **Tall things far out.** The depth range of the camera covers things up to about 700 hole radii tall. A level 1 hole far out in a city can see skyscrapers taller than that cut off at the top. Only reachable by walking very far at a low level.
- **Ring edges are untidy.** Cells never overlap within a ring, but a cell that straddles the line where one ring ends and the next begins holds things only on its own side of it, so a plot is cut off there, and a big thing in one ring can overlap one in the next. Streets that are side streets in one ring end at the ring edge. The ground tiles are coloured from the plot they are in, so they match.
- **The dissolve is generous.** A big thing within 2.2 hole radii of the line between the hole and the camera dissolves, so in a dense city a few buildings near the hole can be faded at once. If that feels like too much, the margin is in `fadeStep()`.
- **Plots are the unit.** An area changes at a cell edge, so the boundary between a village and a forest is a stair of squares.
- **Regions are big, so variety comes later.** The village round the start is at least 150 m in every direction (typically 250 m), and every other region near it is about 400 m or more across, growing with distance. A new player therefore sees only village for the first level 30 or so, and the first forest or city is a few minutes' walk. The knobs are in `REGION` (`FLOOR`, `K`, `R`, `JITTER`) in `src/world.js`.
- **A glitch fixed (25 November 2026):** a hole much bigger than the place it is in (level 400 in a city 700 m out, where things are 7 times the starting size) made and dropped hundreds of map cells every few seconds, so the town seemed to "refresh" itself, and what was eaten there grew back at once. The cause: the list of cells to keep was computed for a larger radius and, for a ring with more than 6,000 cells in range, skipped altogether, while the list of cells to make was not, so those cells were made, dropped at the next look and made again. Now everything wanted is always kept, and a full budget swaps the farthest held cell for a nearer wanted one, never the other way. `scale.test.js` checks it.
- **Bots in the far rings can be bigger than you.** A bot's size is up to 2.6 times what its place suits and it grows to 3 times that, so a hole sitting in a place that matches it will often meet bots bigger than itself and can be swallowed (it loses half its xp). That is the competition; the protection and the speed difference give you a chance to get away. A level 1,000 hole at 500 km can be swallowed by a bigger bot like any other.
- **Bots only exist where cells are loaded and only 12 are active.** A very big hole in a small-scale place sees hundreds of cells but only the 12 nearest bots are drawn and move.
- **Bots do not keep their progress** while you are away (see section 3), and a bot you swallowed returns after 25 seconds.
- **Strips at ring edges.** Where one ring ends and the next begins, the cells of the two sizes decide an area boundary at different resolutions, so a thin strip of an area (one or two cells wide) can occur right along a ring edge (120, 360, 840, 1,800 m ...). It is not a small region and the tests leave it out; fixing it needs a different map structure.
- **Audio was not heard.** `sound.test.js` checks that tones start and nothing errors, not how they sound.
- **Nothing is saved except position, xp and the count.** Eaten things grow back; settings are separate.

## 8. Changes shipped

- **25 November 2026: Quick Game.** A second mode from the title screen: one fixed scale and one cell size everywhere, areas as regions of one size, no level cap, extremely fast growth (level 100 at about half a minute of play), bots and rare things still tougher further out, its own save, and a Quick Mode tag in the HUD. The normal game is unchanged. New checks in `tests/world.test.js` (683 now) and `tests/browser/quick.test.js`. See section 3 for the decisions and the caveat.

- **24 November 2026: first build (solo).** The world rules and map (levels to 1,000, villages, forests and cities with 55 voxel things, streets in cities), the isometric pixel-art renderer, the hole with its pit and ring, eating and falling, steering by mouse, keys and touch, the HUD, the menu and settings, saving, sound, and the tests and tools. Online play and bots are not in it.
- **24 November 2026: the owner's first feedback.** (1) Things beside the hole looked dark: a see-through effect meant for tall buildings was also applied to small things next to the hole, and the outlines were heavy on small things; now only the upper parts (above 1.6 hole radii) of tall things are cut away, and only a quarter of their pixels, the outlines are softer, and the pit's walls are lighter so a falling thing reads. (2) A finer floor: tiles about half the size. (3) Faster growth: a quicker growth curve, a faster hole (6.5 radii a second, was 5.5), more things to eat, and the numbers above. (4) A livelier village: houses (three colours), a cottage, a shed, an apple tree, a horse, a pig, a dog, a cat, ducks, rabbits, a gnome, crates, barrels, fences, hedges, a picnic table and more, with bigger things in sight at the start that come within reach after a few levels; animals now wander. Forests got squirrels and foxes and cities pigeons.
- **24 November 2026: the second round of feedback.** (1) A framework for every area to follow (plots, roads, layouts), with villages, forests and cities rebuilt on it so everything stands where it belongs: cities have a square street grid with pavements, lamps, street trees, bins, traffic lights, cars in the lanes (driving on the left), rows of buildings facing the street and courtyards behind, parks, plazas and car parks; villages have houses facing lanes in fenced gardens with postboxes, farms with paddocks and yards, pastures, fields, orchards, greens and ponds; forests have woods with plants at the foot of the trees, clearings with a cabin and campfire, rocky ground, ponds and groves, with trees kept off trails. New things: terraces, park and street trees, fountain, traffic light, reeds, campfire, woodpile. (2) Mouse steering can be turned off (M, or Settings). (3) A too-big thing dissolves while the hole is under it or it is in the way of the camera. The food density and xp per level were retuned (see section 5).
- **25 November 2026: bigger regions and many more objects.** (1) Areas (village, forest, city) are now regions with a guaranteed minimum size: a jittered grid of region centres on the ring-and-sector map, each with one area, replacing the smooth noise whose patches near the start could be a few metres wide (a tenth of the runs along a line from the start were 16 m or less). The village round the start is at least 150 m in every direction; other regions near it are 400 m or more across and grow with distance. Measured with the widest circle that fits inside each patch, over ten seeds. (2) Many more objects everywhere: the density of everything scattered is 3.2 (was 1.8), street lamps are closer together, more street furniture and traffic, parked cars along every kerb, and denser forests (plants at the foot of every tree, more rocks, bushes, ferns, mushrooms and animals), fields, courtyards, parks, plazas and car parks. Villages are now 130 to 250 things a cell, forests 115 to 160, city blocks about 90. (3) The xp a level needs was raised (0.14 to 0.27) to keep the climb at the pace the owner approved (see section 5).
- **25 November 2026: a refresh glitch fixed.** A very big hole in a small-scale area (a level 400 hole in a city 700 m out) made and dropped hundreds of cells every few seconds, so the area seemed to refresh and what was eaten grew back at once. Cells are no longer made and dropped in turn (see section 7), and a regression test covers it.
- **25 November 2026: rival holes and four new areas.** (1) **Bots**, fixed in the map by the seed: they move, eat and grow by the same rules as you, hunt a hole they can swallow, flee from one that could swallow them and otherwise go for the best thing to eat. A bigger hole swallows a smaller one when its middle is over it and it is about 8% wider. Being swallowed costs half your xp and puts you a little way off, protected; swallowing a bot gives xp, and it returns 25 s later. Names and levels float over each bot (red if it could swallow you, green if you could swallow it), a scoreboard lists the biggest holes near you, the ground is cut out for every hole, and there are sounds for both. Two small, harmless bots live round the start. (2) **Four new areas with the detail of the first three**: Jurassic Park (jungle, dinosaur paddocks with electric fences and gates, nesting grounds, the visitor centre, a lagoon, a laboratory compound), the desert (dunes, an oasis, an adobe town with a market, ruins with a sphinx and a pyramid, a canyon, a nomad camp), the arctic (snowfields, pine woods, frozen lakes with penguins and seals, a research outpost, igloo villages, glaciers) and the volcano (ash plains, lava flows, a crater with a lava lake, mines, a forge, obsidian spires, a whole volcano far out): 24 new kinds of plot, 107 new things, 4 new road styles, and pools of ice and lava and a lava river. Also: an old test that asserted "nothing standing" with things mid-fall was corrected for bots.
